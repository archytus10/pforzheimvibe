# THE LOOP THAT NEVER CONVERGES

**Format:** short video, single presenter to camera
**Runtime:** ~4:05 at a measured 155 wpm (630 words of narration)
**Source material:** MLST #82 — Dr. Joscha Bach, "Digital Physics, DL and Consciousness"
**Transform:** Bach's computational arguments, re-applied to sports psychology

---

## SCRIPT

### [00:00 – 00:21] COLD OPEN

*[VISUAL: tight on face. No music. Let the first line sit.]*

"Am I good enough?"

You've asked yourself that in a tunnel. In a locker room. On a start line at 6am.

And you've noticed something strange. The answer changes every time you check.

That isn't weakness. That isn't a confidence problem.

That's a self-referential statement. And a self-referential statement doesn't have a stable answer.

### [00:21 – 01:00] THE DIAGNOSIS

*[VISUAL: title card — THIS STATEMENT IS FALSE. Hold 2s.]*

Gödel showed this about mathematics a century ago. If you assume truth is something eternal, sitting out there waiting to be found, you can write sentences that flip their own value just by being evaluated. "This statement is false." Check it, and it oscillates. Forever.

A computer doesn't crash on that. It just never converges.

That's your rumination the night before a final. It is not a hard question you keep failing to answer. It's a function that cannot return a value. You can run it for six hours and get nothing out — because there is nothing to get out.

### [01:00 – 01:41] THE FIX

*[VISUAL: warmup footage, mundane and repetitive. Deliberately unglamorous.]*

So drop the assumption.

Truth isn't out there. Truth is whatever a procedure computes. Define the procedure and you get a value. You lose something real — you lose the idea that the answer exists independently of how you asked. What you get back is a machine that runs without hanging.

In practice: "Am I good enough" is unanswerable. "Did I hit my four checkpoints in warmup?" is a procedure. It terminates. It returns a number.

And that number is now what confidence means for you. Not a feeling you audit. An output you compute.

Stop asking the question that never halts. Build the one that does.

### [01:41 – 02:32] YOUR VISUALIZATION IS PROBABLY FAKE

*[VISUAL: split screen — left, a glossy highlight reel; right, raw practice footage from a fixed camera.]*

Second thing. This one costs people medals.

There's a difference between a simulation and a movie.

A movie has no causal structure. It's a sequence of observables. You cannot make a different decision inside a movie.

A game engine has causal structure. Crude physics, different substrate — but you move differently and the world answers differently.

Most mental rehearsal is a movie. The highlight reel. You, winning, crowd noise, slow motion. A sequence of observables with nothing inside it to interact with. It teaches you nothing.

Real rehearsal is a game engine. Rehearse the bad call. Rehearse being down two with legs like wet sand. Then make a decision in there and let the model answer back.

If your visualization can't surprise you, it isn't a model. It's a screensaver.

### [02:32 – 03:13] FEELINGS ARE GEOMETRY

*[VISUAL: simple animated body map. Shapes contracting, rising, pressing. No labels.]*

Third. Listen to how you describe nerves.

Tight. Heavy. Contracting. Something sitting on your chest. Something opening in your legs.

That isn't poetry — it's the only channel available. The system tracking the state of your body can't speak to you in words, so it uses space. It projects into the one space your mind always has running: the map of your own body. Geometry, plus a direction it wants you to move.

So stop reading the label and read the geometry. "I'm anxious" is a guess about a cause. "Something tight, high, and forward" is data.

Only one of those is something you can work with.

### [03:13 – 03:53] AND A WARNING

*[VISUAL: late-race footage. Hold longer than is comfortable.]*

Last one, and it cuts both ways.

The meaning of pain is the avoidance. That's what pain *is* — something upstream of you, changing what you do, that you don't control.

You can get upstream of it. Athletes do. And once you're upstream of your own pain, it stops being pain. It becomes a representation you can move around.

Understand what you just built. You were not meant to get there. Pain is the organism's veto, and you've learned to overrule it.

That's how you finish the race. It's also how you spend the body on your ambition, one season at a time.

### [03:53 – 04:05] CLOSE

*[VISUAL: back to the cold-open framing. Flat, quiet.]*

You're not a person. You're an enormous number of cells behaving coherently enough that it's useful to act as though a person were there.

Train it as the machine it is.

*[END]*

## WHERE EACH BEAT CAME FROM

| Script beat | Bach's original argument |
|---|---|
| The question that never converges | Gödel's self-referential statements: in stateless classical mathematics the truth value flips on self-reference; in a computational system "your computer is not going to crash... the truth value fluctuates in every execution step, it's not going to converge" |
| Confidence as procedure, not fact | "Truth is what you calculate with the following procedure... you just have to make sure that it converges to some kind of value... it's just that you lose your notion that truth is independent of that procedure" |
| Simulation vs. movie | The simulation / simulacrum distinction: "a movie doesn't have causal structure, it only gives you a sequence of observables," versus a game engine that reproduces enough causal structure that "you can make a different decision, make a different move in the game" |
| Feelings are geometry | "Feelings are essentially geometric... the only space that we have always instantiated in our mind is the body map... contracting or expanding, light or heavy... it's all geometry plus valence" — the interface a non-verbal system uses to reach the analytic one |
| Getting upstream of pain | "The semantics of pain are given by the avoidance... when you get upstream of your own pain the pain stops being pain... but we are not meant to get there because it means that we can immunize ourselves to pain and sacrifice the organism to our intellectual interests" |
| Close | "The organism is not a person... it is very useful for the organism to behave as if it was a person" |

## MATERIAL NOT USED (available for follow-ups)

- **Overfitting and grokking** — the practice hero who falls apart in competition is overfit to the training distribution; and the finding that training *past* the overfit sometimes clicks into genuine generalization.
- **Agency from a thermostat** — a thermostat has no agency because it acts only on the present frame; give it the ability to integrate expected outcomes over a branching future and it becomes an agent. Push the depth further and it discovers the idiosyncrasies of its own sensors — which is what an athlete learning to read their own arousal is doing.
- **Spurious correlations** — superstition and ritual as correlations that generalize inside the training set and break under distribution shift.
- **Learning order** — GPT learns style first and semantics last; humans start from salience and relevance. An argument against teaching athletes to *look* like the model before they understand what the movement is for.
- **Executable language of thought** — natural language isn't executable, the internal language of thought is. Why "relax" is a useless cue and a runnable one isn't.
- **"You need a human to guide the human"** — and recursively: does the team actually know where it's going, or is it confusion at every level balancing out?

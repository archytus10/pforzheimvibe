# LEVEL ONE

**Source:** "The Ultimate Guide to Social Intelligence — Most People Are Level 1"
**Applied to:** sports psychology
**Format:** 9:16 vertical, ~60s, self-recorded
**Rule followed:** the source's own Clarity principle — short sentences, no jargon, don't try to sound smart

---

## SCRIPT

### HOOK
> Most athletes are Level 1.
> They think if they work hard, someone will notice.
> That's not humility. That's prey.

### THE JUDGMENT
> Harvard showed people two seconds of silent video of a teacher.
> Their read matched students who sat in that class for a whole semester.
> Two seconds. No sound.
> Your opponent already did that to you in warmups.

### WHAT THEY'RE READING
> They're not reading your stats. They're reading your body.
> Rushing is low status. Filling silence is low status.
> Snapping your head when someone calls your name — low status.
> Yelling is low status. Yelling is what losing control looks like.
> High status is slow. Still. Doesn't explain itself.

### THE FIX
> Slow your walk to the line.
> Pause before you answer a question.
> Keep your head still when you talk.
> That's not confidence. That's behavior.
> You can do it scared.

### BUTTON
> And they can't see the scared part.
> You think it's on your face. It isn't.
> They're too busy hiding their own.

## WHERE IT CAME FROM

| Line | Source |
|---|---|
| "Most athletes are Level 1 / that's prey" | "Most people operate at level one... This is the worldview of prey." |
| The two-second study | Ambady & Rosenthal's thin-slicing experiment — 10s, then 5s, then 2s of silent video matched a full semester's evaluations |
| The low-status list | Keith Johnstone: status is a verb, not a noun — rushing, filling silence, snapping the head, touching the face |
| "Yelling is what losing control looks like" | "Yelling is a low status reaction to a loss of control." |
| "High status is slow. Still." | "They do not rush... They turn their torso slowly... They tolerate silence." |
| "They can't see the scared part" | Epley's illusion of transparency — we think our nerves are visible; they aren't, because everyone is busy hiding their own |

## NOT USED YET

- **Warmth × competence** — the talented transfer nobody passes to. Pure competence with no warmth reads as a threat and the team's immune system attacks it. Probably the single best remaining idea for a team-sport audience.
- **Leakage** — reading a tiring opponent: feet turning toward the exit, hand to the neck, blink rate dropping.
- **Playing low status on purpose** — to get a defensive teammate or a guarded coach to open up.
- **Front stage / backstage** — comparing your own nerves to everyone else's highlight reel.

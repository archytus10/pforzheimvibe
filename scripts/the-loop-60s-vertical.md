# 60-SECOND VERTICAL CUTS

**Format:** 9:16, presenter to camera, burned-in text
**Narrator:** self-recorded — marked up for live delivery, not TTS
**Parent script:** `the-loop-that-never-converges.md`
**Purpose:** cheap hook test before committing to the 4-minute piece
**Measured:** Cut A 161 words + 2 holds = 56-62s · Cut B 163 words + 3 holds = 57-64s
(Both sit at the top of the minute. If a platform caps you at a hard 60, the line to lose is the second half of Cut A's visualization beat, or Cut B's "That's your brain at 2am before a final.")

**Notation:** `/` = short breath · `//` = full stop, ~0.5s · `(hold)` = ~1s of silence
`*stress*` = hit this word · ALL CAPS in the right column = burned-in text

## DELIVERY NOTES

- **First two seconds carry the whole thing.** Both hooks open on a line that sounds like a contradiction. Say it flat — no lift at the end, no "hey guys." Any energy in the first breath reads as an ad.
- **The `(hold)` marks are the content.** In a 60-second cut the silences are what separate the idea from a list of claims. Resist filling them in the edit.
- **Don't punch "Gödel."** Drop it in like a normal word. The moment it sounds like you're impressed by it, the athlete watching leaves.
- **"Four checkpoints" is a placeholder.** Swap in whatever your actual audience does in warmup — it lands much harder as something specific to their sport than as a generic number.
- **Cut A vs Cut B:** A is denser and gives two takeaways; B has a cleaner arc and one. If the metric is retention past 30s, my money's on B — the visualization beat in A arrives right where short-form viewers bail, and it's strong enough to carry a video of its own.
